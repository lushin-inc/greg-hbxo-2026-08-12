Outbound Prospecting

RULE 1

Rebuild the pain menu around the tool they already have

Almost everyone who answers already has something in place, so a generic menu gets "not relevant" every time.

What changes and what doesn't:

  • Pattern interrupt — never changes
  • 30-second commercial — never changes
  • Pain menu — the only part you rewrite

Rebuild it around what's imperfect about the tool they've got:

  • Coverage gaps — projects, regions, or verticals it misses
  • Data that's stale or thin where they need it most
  • Reps who don't actually use it
  • Leads coming in, but not the right business at the right margin

The first 40% of the call is just clearing barriers:

  • The interrupt and the commercial fit round peg, round hole
  • You can already run both in your sleep
  • The pain points are the only part worth your prep time
Where this goes wrong

Running the same generic menu on every call and blaming the list. Your list quality is fine and your answer rate is fine. Only the menu is out of date.

RULE 2

When they brush you off, ask for advice

You don't get real pain points from research — you get them from the people who just told you no.

Say this after they brush you off "All right, I think you know what I do. Can I take the sales hat off and ask your advice for my next call? I promise I won't ask any questions." They say sure. "Based on what I just said — again, advice for my next call — what should I have said?"
Say this if they're happy with what they have "I know you've got a solution you're happy with. What's the thing that could be 10% better?" Then, whatever they say: "Great, thank you so much. Appreciate it." Hang up. Next call.

Use what they tell you on the very next dial:

  • Someone tells you they can't find enough good people
  • Next call opens with "we typically work with people who can't find enough good people — any of that relevant?"
  • You're collecting clues, not pitching
Where this goes wrong

Asking a follow-up question after they give you the answer. You promised you wouldn't. Ask one, and the next twenty people you try this on can smell it.

RULE 3

Interview every customer who switches to you

Someone who just left a competitor hands you the exact sentence that makes the next prospect flinch.

Ask this on every switch you sign for the next six months "What's the reason you guys switched? What was the problem you had before?" Then the one that gets you the real answer: "It probably wasn't terrible. What was the thing that needed to be 10% better, that made you make the leap?"
  • Write it down in their words, not your summary of it
  • Keep a running doc of the answers
  • Those answers become the pain menu in Rule 1

RULE 4

Ask the moment they sign

People are never more excited about a purchase than right after they make it.

  • Rapport is high and they're happy
  • It's before the account gets handed over the fence
  • That's the window — not next quarter
Say this to open the ask "I need your help with something. I'm really trying to get 5 new customers this quarter — I've got 3 so far." Give them a real number. It engages people. "Do you know anybody who …?"

Then describe traits and places, not pain points:

  • They've never heard that person talk about a pain point, so naming one gets you nothing
  • Name what they can actually see — where they run into them, what kind of shop it is, what they're trying to do
  • You're turning a light bulb on. A person pops into their head and they can't control it
Say this to fill in the "anybody who" line "Do you know any business owners that have high integrity, they're in growth mode, and they care about their people? Specifically any software, technology, marketing companies, manufacturing, construction — anything where they need to grow and they need to get leads for their people."
👀 The 20-minute exercise that makes this work — do it before your next ask

Write out your top 10 favorite sales you've ever made. For each one, list every trait that describes that person and that company.

  • The company — size, vertical, how they buy
  • The person — role, how they think, what they're under pressure to do
  • Where someone would run into them — conferences, associations, boards, industry groups

That list is your "do you know anybody who" line. You want to be referred into more people like the ones you already loved selling to.

While you're in there:

  • Check the LinkedIn profiles of your best recent wins for groups, associations, and conferences
  • Ask the live ones straight out: "Are you involved in any professional development groups or associations? Is there a conference you go to every year?"

RULE 5

Turn the name into an introduction

A name is not a referral. This is the line that makes the difference, and almost nobody says it.

Say this the second they give you a name "Awesome, thanks. Do me a favor — do you ever see them at quarterly conferences, or on a Zoom once a month?" Then: "Tell them about your experience with us. Tell them you think they should talk to me, and ask if they're open to it. If they're not, don't worry about it. But if they are, just send a one-line email intro — Greg, meet Bob. Bob, meet Greg. Let's take it from here."

People don't think to do that on their own. Say it out loud and they remember, and they do it.

Where this goes wrong

Taking the name and running with it yourself. A name you cold-call is still a cold call. The introduction — in their words, about their experience — is the whole reason referrals close.

RULE 6

Get on association agendas

You've got NY, NJ and PA within driving distance, which is the whole reason you have that territory.

  • Build a list of associations and industry groups your best accounts belong to
  • Go to the meetings
  • Then get on the agenda — you show up and teach, you don't pitch
  • Count it like prospecting: point a block of dials and LinkedIn messages at booking talks
👀 LinkedIn and the list — what to keep doing, and the one change

Keep doing:

  • Connect with anyone who looks like a strong fit, note kept high-level and human
  • Reference the connection on the next attempt
  • Email behind every call — no reason to let the opportunity slip by

The one change:

  • Load contacts into HubSpot in batches of 50–100, not all 600
  • Work that batch for a week before you add the next

RULE 7

Set the goal on the behavior, never the outcome

The second you set the goal on the result, you've lost it.

  • The goal is that the words come out of your mouth a set number of times
  • Whether anything lands isn't your business that month
  • Dan asks for 7 a month, went three weeks with nothing, then two really good ones out of nowhere
  • Put it on the whiteboard where you can see it — Dan keeps a big 7 on his and marks it off

Tap a target number to change it.

Where this goes wrong

"I'll ask when it feels right." It never feels right. Set the number, mark the tally, and let the outcome take care of itself.

EACH WEEK

The list

  • Load the next 50–100 contacts into HubSpot and work only those
  • Rewrite one pain point using something a real prospect or customer said
  • Use the advice ask at least three times after a brush-off
  • Ask every new signer why they switched, and write it down verbatim
  • Ask for a referral, and close it with the one-line-intro request
  • Add one association or speaking target to the list
  1. Work one batch of 50–100 contacts at a time, not the whole list.
  2. Run the pattern interrupt and the 30-second commercial exactly as written.
  3. Open with pain points about what's imperfect in the tool they already have.
  4. When they brush you off, take the sales hat off and ask their advice for your next call. Get the answer, say thanks, hang up.
  5. Use what they told you on the very next dial.
  6. Interview every customer who switches to you, and feed their words back into step 3.
  7. The moment they sign, ask for the referral: your real number, then traits and places they'd recognize.
  8. When they give you a name, ask them to tell that person about their experience and send a one-line intro email.
  9. Point part of your week at booking talks with the associations your best accounts belong to.
  10. Tally the behaviors on the whiteboard. Ignore the outcome until the month is over.
If you remember nothing else: the pain menu and the referral ask are the only two things you need to get right. Everything else you can already do in your sleep.
